Module 02 · Intuitive Foundations

Physics, Qubits & Quantum Logic Gates

Build crystal-clear mental models before touching matrices. Compare classical bits with quantum states, manipulate the Bloch sphere, and observe entanglement in a live 2-qubit simulator.

1. Classical Bits vs. Qubits

Classical Bit (Deterministic Switch)

A classical bit lives in one of two mutually exclusive states: \(0\) or \(1\). Logic gates (NOT, AND, XOR) map definite bit strings to definite bit strings, as formalized by George Boole and implemented in electrical circuits by Claude Shannon.

\[\text{State} \in \{0, 1\}\]

Qubit (2D Complex Unit Vector)

A qubit is a two-level quantum system whose state is a normalized linear combination of basis states \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\) with complex probability amplitudes \(\alpha, \beta \in \mathbb{C}\):

\[|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle, \qquad |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1\]
Simple Takeaway

A classical bit is a coin resting flat on a table (heads or tails). A qubit is an arrow pointing anywhere on the surface of a 3D sphere—only when you measure it does it snap to the North Pole (\(|0\rangle\)) or South Pole (\(|1\rangle\)) with probability \(|\alpha|^2\) and \(|\beta|^2\).

2. Superposition, Interference & Measurement

Superposition is not "being 0 and 1 at the same time in secret." It means the state vector sits at a diagonal angle between the \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\) axes. When you apply two consecutive Hadamard gates \(H\), the positive and negative amplitudes cancel on \(|1\rangle\) and reinforce on \(|0\rangle\)—that is quantum interference, the engine behind every quantum speedup.

Simple Takeaway

Quantum algorithms work like noise-canceling headphones: they steer probability amplitudes so wrong answers cancel out (destructive interference) and right answers amplify (constructive interference).

Module 03 · Quantum Math Lab

Linear Algebra, Dirac Notation & Unitary Operators

Every quantum circuit is matrix multiplication in a complex Hilbert space. Master the five mathematical pillars from scratch with complete worked derivations and an interactive gate-state calculator.

1. Complex Numbers & Probability Amplitudes

A complex number \(z = a + bi\) (where \(i^2 = -1\)) carries both a magnitude \(|z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\) and a phase \(\theta = \arg(z)\). Its complex conjugate is \(z^* = a - bi\), giving the Born probability \(|z|^2 = z^* z = a^2 + b^2\).

Worked Example: Born Rule Normalization

Verify that \(|\psi\rangle = \frac{1+i}{2}|0\rangle + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|1\rangle\) is a valid quantum state:

\[|\alpha|^2 = \left|\frac{1+i}{2}\right|^2 = \frac{1^2 + 1^2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad |\beta|^2 = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \implies |\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1\]
Simple Takeaway

Complex numbers let amplitudes carry a direction (phase) as well as a size. Squaring the length of an arrow gives its real-world measurement probability.

2. Dirac (Bra-Ket) Notation, Inner & Outer Products

In Dirac notation, a ket \(|v\rangle\) is a column vector, and a bra \(\langle w| = (|w\rangle)^\dagger\) is its conjugate-transpose row vector.

\[|0\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix}, \quad |1\rangle = \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}, \quad \langle i|j\rangle = \delta_{ij}, \quad |0\rangle\langle 1| = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\]

Worked Example: Computing an Inner Product

Given \(|+\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle + |1\rangle)\) and \(|-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|0\rangle - |1\rangle)\):

\[\langle + | - \rangle = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{pmatrix} = \frac{1 - 1}{2} = 0 \quad (\text{orthogonal!})\]
Simple Takeaway

\(\langle a|b\rangle\) is a single number measuring overlap (inner product), whereas \(|a\rangle\langle b|\) is a matrix that transforms states (outer product).

3. Single-Qubit Unitary Matrices & Eigenvalues

Any valid quantum gate must be unitary (\(U^\dagger U = I\)), meaning it preserves vector length and total probability \(1\). The four core single-qubit matrices are:

\[X = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}, \quad Y = \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}, \quad Z = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}, \quad H = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\]

Worked Example: Eigenvalues of Pauli-Z and Hadamard

An eigenvector \(|\lambda\rangle\) satisfies \(U|\lambda\rangle = \lambda|\lambda\rangle\). For \(Z\), \(Z|0\rangle = +1|0\rangle\) and \(Z|1\rangle = -1|1\rangle\), so eigenvalues are \(\pm 1\). For \(H\), \(H|+\rangle = +1|+\rangle\) and \(H|-\rangle = -1|-\rangle\).

Simple Takeaway

Unitary matrices rotate or reflect vectors without stretching or shrinking them. Eigenvectors are the special axes that only get flipped or phase-shifted—never rotated off their line.

4. Tensor Products (Multi-Qubit State Spaces)

Combining two qubits multiplies their dimensions via the Kronecker (tensor) product \(\otimes\), growing exponentially as \(2^n\):

\[|+\rangle \otimes |0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix} \otimes \begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix} = \frac{|00\rangle + |10\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\]
Simple Takeaway

Adding just 1 qubit doubles the vector size. 50 qubits already require \(2^{50} \approx 1.12 \times 10^{15}\) amplitudes—beyond ordinary RAM.

1. Foundational Quantum Algorithms

Bell State & Superdense Coding

Applying \(H\) to qubit 0 followed by \(\text{CNOT}_{0,1}\) prepares \(|\Phi^+\rangle = \frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}\). In superdense coding, Alice applies \(I, X, Z,\) or \(XZ\) to her single entangled qubit to encode 2 classical bits.

Quantum Teleportation

Alice sends an unknown state \(|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle\) to Bob using a shared Bell pair and 2 classical measurement bits. Bob applies conditional \(X\) and \(Z\) corrections to recover \(|\psi\rangle\) faithfully.

Deutsch-Jozsa Algorithm

Determines whether a Boolean oracle \(f(x)\) is constant or balanced in a single query using Hadamard interference and phase kickback onto a \(|-\rangle\) ancilla.

Grover’s Search Amplification

Marks the target state with a phase flip \(O|w\rangle = -|w\rangle\) and reflects amplitudes about the mean via the diffusion operator \(2|s\rangle\langle s| - I\), achieving \(O(\sqrt{N})\) quadratic speedup.
